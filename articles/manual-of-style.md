This is an opinionated style optimizing for legibility and density, written for both humans and LLMs. It's influenced by *The Elements of Style* (Strunk & White) and personal experience.

## Guidelines
- **No hedging:** avoid hedging to make clear, direct, and strong claims.
- **Classic prose style:** write serious arguments in Francis-Noël Thomas & Mark Turner's classic prose style from _Clear and Simple As The Truth_, observational and analytical.
- **Rule of n:** instead of defaulting to the rule of 3, consider the rule of n where n is the sufficient number of terms to express the writer's intent. For example, if 4 items better triangulate the general idea behind 3 items, then use 4.
- **Remove personal titles:** eg "Saint Augustine of Hippo" becomes "Augustine".
- **Inline lists:** lists are inline unless they have >3 items.
- **Parenthetical enumeration:** eg three list items become (1) first, (2) second, and (3) third. This enumeration remains legible inline unlike period enumeration. Sublists use decimal notation, eg (1.1), (1.2), and (1.3).
- **Serial comma:** place a comma before the conjunction in a list of three or more items (eg I have a piano, anvil, and mallet).
- **Logical quotations:** include punctuation in quotation marks, if it appears in the original quotation.
- **Editorial elisions:** ellipsis with whitespace, eg "A … B".
- **Citations:** always footnoted. Format: `[Title](URL) (Surname Year)`. Separate multiple citations within a footnote with semicolons. If no author is given, use the institution name. If neither is given, use `Unknown`. For digital works, usernames count as author names. If no year is given, omit. One author as `John`; 2 authors as `John & Jane`; >=3 authors as `John et al`.
- **Footnote notation:** all superscript footnotes are numbered.
- **Rhetorical colons:** a colon may introduce a list or deliver emphasis.
- **Capitalization after colons:** lowercase when a fragment follows, capitalize when an independent clause follows.
- **Bolded label punctuation:** bold labels followed by colons should bold the colon.
- **Hyphens and dashes:** tightly-coupled compounds use hyphens (eg "adrenaline-dumping fear", "fire-and-forget"). Numerical ranges use en dashes (eg 2020–2024).
- **Binding ampersand:** use "&" to group and disambiguate tightly-related items, eg "The Cold War pitted US & UK against Russia & China."
- **Mathematical substitutions:** may replace quantitative words with their symbol equivalents ("~" for "approximately", "roughly", "about"; ">" for "more than", "over", "above"; "<" for "less than", "under", "below"). Prefer compact units (eg "3h", not "3h0m0s").
- **Large numbers:** digits for the coefficient, spell the scale word (eg "1.2 billion", not "1,200,000,000").
- **Ranges with units:** unit on the final value only (eg "5–10kg"), unless ambiguity results.
- **Dates:** prose format (eg "15 June 2024").
- **Acronyms, initialisms, and Latin abbreviations remove periods:** eg "USA", not "U.S.A."; "AI", not "A.I."
- **No diaeresis:** naïve should be naive.
- **Italics:** use for emphasis, neologisms, titles of works, and foreign phrases not found in standard English dictionaries.
- **Sentence case:** article titles and section headers are in sentence case.
